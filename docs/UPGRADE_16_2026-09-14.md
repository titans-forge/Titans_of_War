## Research and architectural limits

The [NPS Heater House history](https://www.nps.gov/places/heater-house.htm) identifies Solomon and Caroline Heater's home and describes fighting reaching the property during the morning attack, followed by the afternoon retreat. Its contemporary photograph was inspected for the main block, lower wing, clapboard walls and chimneys. Roof covering, porch and yard details are interpretive; this is not a measured 1864 restoration.

The [NPS Chancellorsville tour](https://www.nps.gov/frsp/learn/photosmultimedia/chancellorsville-battlefield-tour.htm) describes the working ironworks and Jackson's May 2 march past it. The [NPS surviving-stack photograph](https://www.nps.gov/places/catharine-furnace-ruins.htm) was inspected for masonry and shape. The upper stack, shed and charging ramp are an illustrative reconstruction, not a verified engineering plan. The game shows the wartime industrial site, not a copy of today's capped ruin.

Both locations, distances, cultivated plots and connecting tracks serve compressed tactical boards. They are not surveyed coordinates or exact historical road traces. The search-indexed Cedar Creek nomination and furnace monograph could not be retrieved in full; neither is claimed as inspected architectural evidence. A conflicting modern-highway-side description was deliberately excluded from the notes.

Two original 1536 × 1024 RGBA PNGs were produced with the built-in image generator and copied without pixel edits: 4,980,493 bytes total. See [art provenance and alpha/hash checks](historical-landmark-art-v6.json) and [complete generation prompts](historical-landmark-prompts-v6.json). Reference screenshots remain in the task's external evidence folder.

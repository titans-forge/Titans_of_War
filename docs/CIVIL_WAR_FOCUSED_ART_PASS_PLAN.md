## Executive decision

Gettysburg will be the vertical slice for the entire art pass. It already exercises the broadest useful combination of infantry, officers, standards, artillery, close combat, retreat, open terrain, roads, walls, farms, and historically identifiable structures. The team will bring Gettysburg to the target standard first, test it at actual playing scale, and freeze a reusable art language before changing the other battlefields.

The pass will proceed in this order:

## Target result

The game should look like a deliberately art-directed historical battlefield viewed from a consistent elevated camera. It should not look like units pasted onto terrain, an assembly of unrelated generated images, a board game covered by permanent boxes, or a collection of rigid formation stamps.

The target is realistic 2D rather than an imitation of 3D. The art should use the strengths of 2D: readable silhouettes, controlled composition, strong period color, fine close-up detail, and consistent visual hierarchy. Every asset must work at three scales:

1. **Close inspection:** soldiers, weapons, crews, flags, and landmarks withstand a promotional close-up.
2. **Normal play:** movement, firing, melee, retreat, morale state, side, and formation facing are immediately readable.
3. **Operational overview:** terrain lanes, objectives, landmarks, exits, and major force groupings remain legible without permanent unit boxes.

The internal goal is ten-out-of-ten craft. Promotion from one battlefield to the next requires objective acceptance checks rather than a subjective score alone.

## Non-negotiable art rules

1. **Historical plausibility:** Uniform cuts, headgear, flags, weapons, artillery, structures, vegetation, fortifications, and material culture must fit the battle and date. Generated art is a draft, not historical evidence.
2. **One camera:** Units, guns, trees, buildings, fences, smoke, and ground features must share the same elevated three-quarter perspective and horizonless battlefield projection.
3. **One light:** Each board gets a documented sun direction, shadow softness, weather, and time-of-day treatment. Assets may not carry contradictory baked lighting.
4. **One scale system:** People, horses, guns, houses, trees, roads, fences, and battlefield distances need a stable apparent scale. Gameplay footprints remain authoritative even when art extends beyond them.
5. **Human variation without noise:** Formations use controlled differences in pose, spacing, stance, equipment, and animation phase. Variation must never disguise side, facing, readiness, or cohesion.
6. **No permanent unit cards on the ground:** Names and detailed information appear through hover, focus, selection, or an accessible inspection state. The field may use restrained selection, facing, morale, and objective cues when needed.
7. **Flags behave like cloth:** The pole remains stable; the fabric sags, ripples, curls, and reacts to movement. Battle-appropriate designs remain recognizable at play scale.
8. **Effects serve state:** Smoke, dust, muzzle flash, sparks, debris, casualties, and hit reactions communicate events. They do not obscure command information for extended periods.
9. **Art cannot silently change rules:** Decorative boundaries do not become collision, cover, concealment, or movement modifiers. Any new physical obstruction requires an explicit mechanics decision and tests.
10. **Performance is part of finish:** A sharper image that causes delayed interaction, texture pressure, or unstable frame pacing has not passed the art review.
11. **Accessibility remains intact:** Reduced-motion behavior, keyboard focus, readable contrast, and non-color state cues survive the pass.
12. **Every asset is traceable:** Source references, generation prompts, edits, dimensions, rights, version, and final hash belong in a manifest.

## Phase 1 — Gettysburg vertical slice

### 1. Establish the Gettysburg baseline

Before producing art, capture the current board in a repeatable matrix:

- opening deployment before orders;
- major formations moving in line and column;
- infantry aiming and firing;
- artillery limbered, deploying, firing, and recovering;
- a close-combat collision;
- a formation rallying;
- an orderly fallback;
- a routed retreat and field exit;
- Leister House and Abraham Brian House at overview, normal, and close zoom;
- Union and Confederate standards stationary and moving;
- full interface, hover-only information, selected unit, and no selected unit;
- desktop wide, 1280 × 720, narrow desktop, and representative phone layout;
- normal motion and reduced motion.

Record the build identifier, scenario choice, viewport, zoom, formation positions, and capture time in a Gettysburg shot manifest. Keep these captures for direct before-and-after comparison.

### 2. Write the Gettysburg art-direction sheet

The sheet must settle the following before image generation:

- elevated three-quarter camera angle and apparent focal length;
- soldier, horse, gun, tree, fence, and building scale ratios;
- sun direction, shadow length, sky condition, and summer color temperature;
- Union and Confederate uniform palettes, with accepted controlled variation;
- ground colors for trampled grass, cultivated plots, road dust, exposed soil, and stone;
- material references for wool, leather, wood, iron, stone, canvas, and flag fabric;
- smoke colors and opacity by age;
- permitted outline, edge, and sharpening treatment;
- minimum readable detail at normal play zoom;
- examples of rejected traits: anime faces, glossy plastic fabric, oversized weapons, duplicate poses, malformed hands, fake lettering, modern construction details, and cinematic lighting inconsistent with the board.

This sheet becomes the visual contract for later battles. Later boards may change season, weather, vegetation, uniforms, and structures while retaining its camera, scale, rendering, and state language.

### 3. Upgrade soldiers and formations

Work from large source figures, then produce tested atlases rather than generating entire formations as single images.

Required Gettysburg figure families:

- Union and Confederate infantry at ready;
- advancing and double-quick movement;
- aiming and firing;
- reloading and recovering from recoil;
- close-combat thrust, parry, and struggle silhouettes;
- rallying, wavering, exhausted, and retreating postures;
- officers directing, observing, and rallying;
- standard bearers at rest, moving, and under pressure;
- artillery crew roles: load, ram, aim, fire, recoil response, and service recovery.

Each family needs restrained head, torso, arm, equipment, and spacing variants. Variation should be deterministic so save/resume and repeated captures do not visibly shuffle a formation. Animation should use enough phase variation to remove the synchronized marching-toy effect while keeping feet and facing coherent.

Formation composition should visually respond to losses. A formation that has lost strength should contain fewer visible soldiers and wider or irregular spacing. Loss display must remain bounded so game strength can still be judged and tiny formations do not disappear prematurely.

### 4. Upgrade flags and standards

Gettysburg sets the flag system for all later battles:

- verify designs and date applicability before final rendering;
- provide a stable pole and finial with side-appropriate material treatment;
- animate cloth through a small frame set or segmented deformation with sag, traveling folds, edge curl, and movement response;
- vary phase and amplitude gently between formations;
- reduce motion without freezing the flag into a rigid rectangle;
- avoid mirrored lettering, invented devices, impossible star counts, and symbols that collapse at play scale;
- attach the standard visually to a bearer rather than floating above an unrelated formation block;
- check silhouettes against smoke, trees, grass, and both army palettes.

The standard may remain slightly more saturated than nearby uniforms because it is a command cue, but it must share the scene's light and wear.

### 5. Upgrade artillery and crew integration

Artillery must read as a working team rather than a gun sprite with nearby people:

- consistent gun and limber scale;
- believable wheel contact and shadow;
- crews positioned by task;
- recoil followed by recovery, smoke, and crew movement;
- visible difference between moving, unlimbering, ready, firing, suppressed, and withdrawing;
- horse or limber treatment where the current state calls for it;
- no crew member intersecting wheels, barrels, landmarks, or neighboring batteries;
- directional muzzle flash and smoke aligned with the barrel.

### 6. Rebuild the Gettysburg environment as a coherent place

The Gettysburg terrain should join its features into one field rather than display isolated decorative objects.

Required work:

- unify grass, worn ground, road, field, wall, fence, and farm palettes;
- remove visible tiling and abrupt texture seams;
- connect the Leister House and Abraham Brian House to roads, paths, fences, yards, cultivation, outbuildings, and trampled approaches supported by research;
- distinguish passable decoration from blocking walls and buildings;
- give every physical obstacle an accurate visible footprint;
- add restrained verge, rut, dust, crop, and damage variation without cluttering movement lanes;
- use trees and hedges in groups with scale and color variation, not repeated stamps;
- reserve high-frequency detail for close inspection while keeping normal play calm enough for unit silhouettes;
- verify that landmark art never hides an objective, route, exit, or selected formation.

### 7. Refine battlefield effects

Effects should have a beginning, readable peak, and decay:

- brief warm muzzle flashes with scale appropriate to rifles and guns;
- expanding smoke that starts dense, rises or drifts consistently, thins, and clears command sightlines;
- restrained dust tied to dry movement and artillery discharge;
- short impact puffs, sparks, and debris matched to soil, wood, stone, or metal;
- a brief readable hit response that does not resemble an arcade damage aura;
- close-combat contact accents synchronized with animation and sound events;
- retreat dust and disorder cues that stop after units leave the field;
- casualty presentation that remains respectful, historically serious, and legible without graphic excess.

### 8. Remove visual interface competition

The battlefield should carry the visual focus. During the Gettysburg pass:

- keep unit names and detailed numbers on hover, keyboard focus, or selection;
- retain a clear selected-unit treatment without an opaque permanent box;
- keep facing, destination, order, morale, suppression, and retreat information available through compact field cues and the command panel;
- prevent labels from covering flags, landmarks, neighboring labels, or objective text;
- scale cues by zoom so the overview is not covered in close-up detail;
- confirm that hiding persistent labels does not reduce keyboard or touch discoverability;
- preserve high-contrast non-color indicators for urgent states.

### 9. Optimize only after the approved look is stable

Keep high-resolution sources outside the shipping path. Export production atlases and scene assets to explicit budgets, then measure:

- compressed bytes loaded for first Gettysburg interaction;
- decoded texture memory;
- time to first controllable frame;
- active-combat frame-time percentiles;
- long-session memory behavior;
- effect density during the heaviest artillery and melee state.

Prefer atlas reuse, crop removal, correct dimensions, deferred loading, texture reuse, and lossless or visually verified encoding. Do not blur approved close-up art merely to hit an arbitrary file-size target.

## Gettysburg promotion gate

No later battlefield begins its final production pass until Gettysburg meets all of these conditions:

- [ ] Art-direction sheet approved and stored with references.
- [ ] All required unit states readable at normal play zoom.
- [ ] No obvious synchronized formation motion during a sustained advance.
- [ ] Visible strength declines as formations take losses.
- [ ] Officers, standard bearers, and gun crews read as distinct roles.
- [ ] Both armies' flags are historically reviewed, cloth-like, and correctly attached.
- [ ] Leister House and Abraham Brian House belong to connected, researched surroundings.
- [ ] Terrain has no obvious seams, scale conflicts, or repeated-stamp pattern at normal zoom.
- [ ] Fire, smoke, dust, impact, melee, rally, and retreat effects communicate state without obscuring play.
- [ ] Detailed information appears on inspection; the idle field is not covered by unit boxes.
- [ ] Selection, objectives, routes, exits, and dangerous states remain immediately readable.
- [ ] Normal and reduced-motion modes both look intentional.
- [ ] Desktop, narrow desktop, and touch layouts preserve the playable field and controls.
- [ ] Landmark collision and line-of-fire behavior match their visible footprints.
- [ ] Gettysburg mechanics, visual, asset, save/resume, and build checks pass.
- [ ] A before-and-after capture set is reviewed at close, play, and overview scale.
- [ ] No measured startup, memory, or frame-time regression remains unexplained.

If a candidate looks excellent in a crop but weak at actual play zoom, it does not pass.

### 10. Cedar Creek

## Production workflow for every battlefield

Every battlefield follows the same sequence:

1. **Capture the baseline.** Use the common state, zoom, motion, and viewport matrix.
2. **Build a research packet.** Record authoritative maps, photographs or drawings, material references, uniforms, standards, structures, vegetation, season, weather, and reconstruction caveats.
3. **Write a board-specific art sheet.** Preserve the shared camera and scale while specifying palette, light, terrain, atmosphere, landmark treatment, and unique effects.
4. **Map issues to asset families.** Identify what can reuse the shared library, what needs a variant, and what requires a new asset.
5. **Generate or source candidates.** Produce isolated, reviewable candidates rather than a whole battlefield baked into one image.
6. **Perform human cleanup.** Correct anatomy, weapons, hands, faces, lettering, seams, alpha edges, shadows, and material inconsistencies.
7. **Integrate behind a versioned selector.** Keep the accepted prior asset available for direct comparison and rollback.
8. **Review at close, play, and overview scale.** Do not approve from source-resolution images alone.
9. **Test states and permutations.** Movement, fire, melee, retreat, rally, casualties, reinforcements, weather or phase changes, labels, selection, and reduced motion.
10. **Validate semantics.** Confirm collision, cover, concealment, line of fire, roads, objectives, exits, and landmark footprints.
11. **Optimize and measure.** Repack only after approval, then compare bytes, memory, startup, and frame time.
12. **Freeze the board.** Archive captures, prompts, manifests, hashes, sources, known limitations, and test results before moving on.

## Image-generation and asset pipeline

The upgraded image model can accelerate production, but it should sit inside a controlled art pipeline:

### Candidate generation

- Start from the battle art sheet and a single asset role.
- Request a consistent elevated three-quarter view, neutral or transparent background, fixed light, complete silhouette, and historically specified equipment.
- Generate multiple candidates with one controlled variable at a time.
- Never ask the model to render readable flag text, unit names, or factual labels into the art.
- Keep source-resolution outputs and prompt metadata outside the shipping bundle.

### Candidate rejection

Reject an image for any of the following:

- incorrect period equipment or flag design;
- malformed hands, faces, feet, weapons, wheels, harness, or architecture;
- mixed camera angles or contradictory shadows;
- glossy, toy-like, anime, painterly, or cinematic treatment outside the art sheet;
- figures cropped through required animation joints;
- false transparency, halos, baked scenery, or unusable contact shadows;
- a silhouette that fails at normal play size;
- a visual that cannot be animated or varied without obvious warping.

### Cleanup and export

- preserve an editable master;
- remove background and edge contamination manually where needed;
- normalize scale, light, color, sharpness, and ground contact;
- split animation-ready limbs or frames only after anatomy approval;
- set consistent pivot, anchor, crop, padding, and frame dimensions;
- export a lossless master and a measured delivery asset;
- update the manifest with source, prompt, tool/model, edits, rights status, dimensions, hash, and destination;
- run asset tests before the visual review.

## Historical research packet required per board

No named feature or date-specific standard should be added solely because it looks appropriate. Each board's packet should include:

- battle date and playable time window;
- documented weather, light, season, and ground condition where available;
- period map and modern orientation map, clearly distinguished;
- playable sector boundaries and the game's representative-coordinate disclaimer;
- primary or authoritative evidence for each named structure or feature;
- whether a surviving structure is original, reconstructed, relocated, or a later replacement;
- road, fence, field, orchard, wood, creek, ridge, earthwork, and settlement character;
- documented army and major unit uniform or equipment differences;
- flag applicability and uncertainty;
- artillery types and visible equipment needed by the scenario;
- civilian, farm, camp, industrial, or siege context;
- areas where visual inference is necessary;
- source URLs, access dates, license or use status, and concise notes.

Historical research approves what should be depicted. Art direction decides how it should look. Gameplay code decides what it does.

## Visual QA matrix

Each completed board must be reviewed across the following matrix:

| Dimension | Required cases |
|---|---|
| Zoom | Close promotional view; normal play; full operational overview |
| Viewport | Wide desktop; 1280 × 720; narrow desktop; representative phone portrait and landscape |
| Motion | Normal; reduced motion; paused |
| Selection | None; hover/focus; selected friendly; inspected enemy; multi-unit command state |
| Formation state | Ready; line; column; skirmish or deep state where supported; moving; aiming; firing; recovering |
| Combat state | Musket exchange; artillery fire; close combat; suppression; rally; retreat; route; field exit |
| Density | Isolated unit; adjacent friendly formations; crossed lines; maximum intended on-screen force |
| Environment | Landmark foreground; trees; road; wall/fence; smoke; dust; objective; edge/exit |
| Side | Union and Confederate assets in every shared state |
| Continuity | Fresh battle; saved checkpoint; resumed checkpoint; long session; battle result return |

Reviewers should answer five questions for every capture:

1. What is happening?
2. Which way is each formation facing and moving?
3. Which side, type, and state does each unit represent?
4. What terrain or structure matters here?
5. Is any visual detail implying a rule that the game does not have?

Any answer that requires opening a debug panel indicates unfinished visual communication.

After the shared library freezes, after every three completed boards, and before release:

## Risk register

| Risk | Early warning | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Generated assets drift in style | Different faces, outlines, light, or perspective between roles | Approve the Gettysburg art sheet first; normalize assets; reject rather than patch severe drift. |
| Better close-ups become worse gameplay pieces | Detail vanishes or turns noisy at normal zoom | Review every candidate at final rendered size before atlas production. |
| Formations still look cloned | Synchronized gait and identical silhouettes remain obvious | Use deterministic pose, spacing, equipment, and phase variation within strict readability limits. |
| Flags remain rigid or become distracting | Rectangular motion, excessive waving, or unreadable emblems | Separate pole and cloth; cap amplitude; test stationary, moving, and reduced-motion states. |
| New scenery creates false tactics | Art suggests cover or blocked movement where none exists | Mark decorative versus mechanical features in the board sheet and test footprints explicitly. |
| Landmarks become pasted illustrations | Perspective, light, scale, or ground contact differs from terrain | Generate isolated masters, relight and grade them, then build connected surroundings in-engine. |
| Texture growth harms loading | Per-board preload or decoded memory rises sharply | Reuse atlases, load by board, crop, repack, and measure before promotion. |
| Smoke and foliage obscure play | Units or objectives disappear during peak effects | Apply density and lifetime caps; protect selected-unit and objective layers. |
| Historical confidence exceeds evidence | Precise-looking but unsupported structure, flag, or terrain detail | Require source notes and label reconstructed, representative, or alternate-history elements. |
| Completed boards drift after shared changes | A later improvement breaks Gettysburg or Manassas | Maintain fixed comparison scenes and rerun them whenever a shared asset changes. |

## Scoring rubric

Score each category from 1 to 10 after the board passes functional checks. A board needs at least 9 in every category and no unresolved critical defect before it is frozen. Ten requires no meaningful improvement visible within the current 2D direction and performance budget.

| Category | What a 10 means |
|---|---|
| Historical visual credibility | Period, place, material, uniforms, standards, and structures withstand informed review; uncertainty is documented. |
| Unit art | Soldiers, officers, standard bearers, and crews look human, grounded, varied, and coherent close up and at play scale. |
| Animation | Motion is readable, weighty, unsynchronized, state-specific, and free of sliding or obvious warping. |
| Flags | Correct, cloth-like, integrated with bearers, visible without dominating, and convincing in reduced motion. |
| Terrain and scenery | The board is a coherent place with strong identity, scale, light, and connected surroundings. |
| Effects | Fire, smoke, dust, impact, melee, retreat, and damage are convincing, restrained, and informative. |
| Tactical readability | Side, facing, state, objective, route, exit, and selection are clear without permanent battlefield boxes. |
| Interface integration | Labels and controls appear when needed, avoid collisions, and do not compete with the field. |
| Performance | Approved art meets recorded startup, memory, and frame-time targets on named target devices. |
| Consistency | The board belongs to the same game as every accepted battlefield while retaining its own identity. |

## Definition of done for the focused art pass

The complete pass is done only when:

- Gettysburg passed first and its shared art contract is documented;
- all eleven playable battlefield configurations have completed the common workflow;
- every board has at least one or two defensible historically identifiable anchors appropriate to its playable geography, with representative features clearly labeled;
- unit families cover movement, firing, artillery service, melee, rally, exhaustion, retreat, and route;
- formations visibly and coherently decline with losses;
- standards are battle-appropriate and animate as cloth;
- no board depends on permanent unit boxes for basic recognition;
- close, normal, and overview scales all pass review;
- reduced motion, keyboard focus, touch inspection, and narrow layouts remain usable;
- visual footprints agree with collision and line-of-fire behavior;
- manifests, prompts, references, rights, hashes, and fallbacks are complete;
- target performance is measured on named devices and regressions are resolved or explicitly accepted;
- targeted and full validation pass;
- a cross-battle capture reel shows consistent quality without hiding weak boards;
- the final release artifact is built and verified locally;
- publication remains a separate, explicit action.

## Immediate starting checklist

The first working session should execute these items in order:

1. Create the Gettysburg baseline capture manifest and reproduce its fixed scenes.
2. Inventory every Gettysburg-loaded unit, standard, terrain, landmark, scenery, effect, and interface asset.
3. Measure current Gettysburg compressed bytes, decoded texture memory, first interaction, and active-combat frame time.
4. Build the Gettysburg historical reference packet.
5. Write and approve the one-page Gettysburg art-direction sheet.
6. Produce a small comparison set: one Union infantry source figure, one Confederate infantry source figure, one officer, one standard bearer, one artillery crew role, one flag cloth test, one tree group, and one ground patch.
7. Integrate those candidates behind a versioned Gettysburg-only selector.
8. Compare them at close, play, and overview scale in normal and reduced motion.
9. Reject or correct the weak parts before generating complete families.
10. Complete Gettysburg in the Phase 1 order: people, flags, artillery, environment, effects, interface integration, optimization, and promotion review.
11. Freeze the shared art library.
12. Move to the alternate Gettysburg board, then follow the numbered battlefield order at the top of this document.

The first tangible milestone is therefore not a folder full of generated soldiers. It is an approved Gettysburg comparison scene that proves the new people, flag, terrain, and lighting language works together at actual play scale.

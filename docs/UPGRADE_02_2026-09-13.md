# Civil War upgrade 02 — historical landmarks — 0.4.0

The first three landmark battles now have recognizable buildings that affect movement and direct fire. This begins the accepted plan for one or two historical landmarks per battle.

## Delivered

| Battle | Landmark | Art and setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First Manassas | Henry House | Original modest frame farmhouse, informed by the NPS 1861 drawing; weathered clapboard, shingled gable, brick chimney. |
| Third Winchester | Hackwood | Stone house and dependencies on the northern flank, informed by the house illustration in the user's battlefield-sign photograph. |
| Cedar Creek | Belle Grove | Low limestone mansion with a raised basement, hipped roof, portico and four chimneys beside the headquarters sector. |

Each sprite has original transparency, a worn yard and a short approach. Optional mission labels name the house. The terrain overlay outlines its occupied ground. Expand **Battlefield landmarks** in the formation inspector for historical context and a source link; this also works through **Formation details** on phones.

Buildings block movement and direct fire equally for both armies. Pathfinding routes infantry and guns around them. Swept movement checks prevent tunneling across the footprint. Turning clearance is reserved around each house, including both artillery travel and deployed dimensions. Individual and group move previews reject occupied destinations before issuing couriers. A formation cannot widen into a building or into an obstructed queued move destination. Reinforcements cannot spawn inside a footprint, and melee cannot cross it.

Yards and short paths are decorative; they grant no additional cover or movement bonus. There is no garrison, healing, building destruction, or stone-wall mechanic in this pass.

## Save compatibility

Fresh campaigns and quick battles use battle rules 8. Save envelope 12 stores the new rules. Existing envelopes 1–11 keep their recorded battle version. Before starting an older battle, the player can explicitly choose **Use updated battle mechanics** for that battle; earlier results remain unchanged. Command training retains its existing rules.

## Historical evidence and limits

Henry House follows the original building shown in the [NPS 1861 drawing and history](https://home.nps.gov/mana/learn/kidsyouth/hugh-fauntleroy-henry.htm). The [present Henry House](https://www.nps.gov/places/000/henry-house.htm) is a postwar replacement. The sprite portrays the original house standing; destruction during the battle is not simulated.

Hackwood's battlefield role is supported by [Shenandoah Valley Battlefields Foundation](https://www.shenandoahatwar.org/third-winchester-article-1) and the [American Battlefield Trust afternoon battle map](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/maps/battle-third-winchester-300-400pm). Architectural detail uses the user's interpretive-sign photograph. Ambiguous features are illustrative; this is not a measured reconstruction.

Belle Grove's history is documented by [the site](https://bellegrove.org/about/history) and [NPS](https://home.nps.gov/places/belle-grove-plantation.htm). Its broad roof, limestone construction and four chimneys follow the [historic landmark description](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/31491367-4395-4447-ac68-e25a8e851107). Porch details remain interpretive.

Building scale, rectangular occupied ground and positions are representative placements on compressed game boards, not survey coordinates. All three assets were generated with the built-in OpenAI image tool and copied unchanged; the game scales and composites them at runtime. Exact prompts are in `historical-landmark-prompts-v1.json`; original dimensions, hashes, sources and limitations are in `historical-landmark-art-v1.json`. Only this first batch receives the new physical rules; existing decorative structures elsewhere are unchanged.

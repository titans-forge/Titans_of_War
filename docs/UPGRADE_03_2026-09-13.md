# Civil War upgrade 03 — three more battlefield landmarks — 0.5.0

Shiloh, Chancellorsville and Chickamauga now have distinctive historical buildings with physical movement and firing constraints. This extends the first batch's landmark system to six of the eleven playable battle scenarios. Petersburg's existing Crater remains in place; the full landmark plan is still open.

## Delivered

| Battle | Building | Setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shiloh | Shiloh Church | Small log chapel behind the forward camp line, with a worn yard and approach. |
| Chancellorsville | Chancellor House | Large brick headquarters house east of Hooker's center, standing during the May 2 scenario. |
| Chickamauga | Brotherton Cabin | Modest log dwelling beside the breakthrough sector, east of the road with room for troops to pass. |

All three PNGs were generated with the built-in OpenAI image tool and copied unchanged with original transparency. Exact selected and rejected prompts are in `historical-landmark-prompts-v2.json`; dimensions, hashes, sources and historical limitations are in `historical-landmark-art-v2.json`. The tool did not report an exact model identifier. Painted checkerboard exports and one broad background halo were rejected, not included in the game.

Buildings use the established rules for obstructing movement and direct fire equally for both armies. Infantry and artillery route around the occupied ground with turning clearance. Individual and group move previews reject blocked destinations. The tactical overlay shows the footprint; the formation inspector provides the building name, context and historical reference. Yards and approaches are decorative. Garrisoning, destruction and additional wall-cover mechanics remain outside this batch.

## Historical basis

The [NPS Shiloh Church tour stop](https://www.nps.gov/places/shiloh-church-tour-stop-5.htm) identifies the log meeting house and Sherman's defense of the ridge on April 6, 1862. The modest chapel design is informed by the park's A. M. Connett painting, dated circa 1901. That painting is a later interpretation, not a wartime photograph.

Chancellor House uses the main house in [Edwin Forbes's May 1, 1863 drawing at the Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2004661922/) and the [NPS site history](https://www.nps.gov/places/chancellor-house-site.htm). The game depicts the house standing during May 2; its destruction occurred on May 3. Fine porch, dormer and rear-wing details remain interpretive.

The [NPS Brotherton farm account](https://www.nps.gov/chch/learn/news/chickamaugaartilleryapril29.htm) places the farm in Longstreet's September 20 breakthrough. The [Historic Resource Study appendix](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/chch/hrs/appendixb.pdf) dates the surviving cabin to 1867 and describes a roughly 21 by 20 foot log dwelling, side-gabled roof and exterior stone chimney. [Chapter 1](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/chch/hrs/chapter1.pdf) explains the uncertainty about early building appearance. Accordingly, both the in-game note and art manifest call this an illustrative reconstruction; it is not presented as an exact surviving 1863 structure.

Scale, footprints and placements serve compressed playable boards, not surveyed geographic reconstruction. Reference images are retained in task evidence, not shipped as game assets.

# Petersburg siege-site art — 0.17.0

The night sortie's Western Siege Train and Eastern Engineer Depot previously used generic artillery presentation. They now read as working material yards: covered and open wagons, horses and teamsters in the west; tools, gabions, fascines and engineer laborers in the east. Small worn tracks join both sites to the existing central supply road.

Four new native PNG assets provide intact and damaged states. A recorded raid switches each site to an abandoned, damaged scene, with the people removed and restrained smoke and embers. Sites and their View buttons stay anchored to the original objective coordinates even after the underlying simulation unit withdraws. Mission markers gain a check and “Struck” label. Missing either member of an intact/damaged pair retains the existing formation renderer for that target.

This is presentation work. Battle rules remain 15 and the save envelope remains 19. Combat, target detection, road speed, cover, outcome grading and campaign behavior have not been changed. The thin yard tracks are decorative; the briefing explicitly distinguishes them from tactical movement and cover.

## Historical scope and art provenance

These are illustrative material yards for the game's abstract sortie objectives, not surveyed reconstructions or two newly verified historical buildings. The [National Park Service account of engineering the Washington defenses](https://home.nps.gov/cwdw/learn/historyculture/engineering-the-defenses.htm) describes material depots, engineer labor, gabions and fascines. That general context does not establish these Petersburg locations or their exact appearance. The existing Crater remains Petersburg's historical terrain landmark.

Generation used the built-in `image_gen.imagegen` tool. Native 1536 × 1024 RGBA files were copied unchanged, totaling 11,888,389 bytes. Alpha-channel statistics and source/output SHA-256 values are recorded in [the art manifest](siege-target-art-v1.json); complete prompts are in [the prompt record](siege-target-prompts-v1.json). Three edit attempts were rejected because they produced RGB images with opaque black/checkerboard backgrounds. The final damaged images were regenerated independently: object layouts are similar but are not pixel-registered to the intact versions. No local pixel-editing pipeline was used.

# Gettysburg v14: native Confederate south-facing movement and actions

Prepared October 3, 2026 local time. The local `?artPass=gettysburg-v14` Gettysburg preview adds Confederate A south-facing four-phase marching and ready/fire/two-state loading. It selects thirty-two atlas pairs and sixty-four clips, retaining every v13 selected atlas, clip, older policy/pose and historical author/render helper exactly. This is a local opt-in art preview, with incomplete family coverage and no default or production promotion.

## What improved

Troops turning south during an active rout or withdrawal now have matching front-facing native art instead of the accepted fallback view. Deliberate south-facing holds and moves also have matching weapon and gait poses. Fighting fallback deliberately keeps facing the enemy while moving backward; that remains intact. A withdrawal or rout suppresses firing. Gettysburg results freeze tactical state rather than creating a new resolved retreat; this pass preserves that behavior.

The four march poses were inspected as full hip-to-knee-to-shin-to-boot chains: left contact, left support/right passing, right contact, right support/left passing. Two earlier whole-sheet attempts repeated supporting legs and failed solid padding; neither was installed. A new native colored-limb structural guide clarified opposite supporting legs for generation. Its colors, measurements and scale are not reused as final uniform pixels or anatomical certification.

The final gray/tan uniform sheets show worn wool, natural trouser folds, leather straps, equipment and a front-facing soldier with restrained leg lift. Broad identity/kit continuity is plausible; exact face, skeleton, camera calibration, equipment manufacture and historical drill are not established. Per-cell concealed-pelvis X estimates are projected to separately measured supporting boot soles, rather than assuming a row's lowest pixel is ground. Ready/loading weapon butts extend slightly below a boot and are excluded from ground measurement. Each whole source uses one rounded median cap-to-sole reference height, preserving actual pose variation.

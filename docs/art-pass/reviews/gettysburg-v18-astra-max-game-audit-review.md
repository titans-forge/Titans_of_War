## Assessment

The revised **7.8/10 provisional overall estimate** is defensible as an editorial judgment, within a reasonable 7.5–8.0 range. The original 8.3 estimate, including 8.6 for historical/educational presentation, overstated the evidence after actual campaign contradictions were found. The author accepted the lower assessment and added the independently found defects to the repair priorities. No whole-game category has demonstrated an unqualified 9/10 yet.

The game has useful tactical substance: formation support, ammunition, morale, contact and organized extraction can matter; goals and outcomes can be inspected; battle saves restore meaningful state. Its strongest evidence here is the full Gettysburg encounter ending in an orderly five-formation withdrawal. The principal shortcomings are campaign causality and explanation, incomplete visual families, laborious command recovery, substantial texture loading and unmeasured experience on target devices. Adding more isolated sprites will not by itself resolve those shortcomings.

The revised category estimates—mechanics 8.2, whole-game visuals 7.5, controls 8.0, historical/educational presentation 7.5, replay/balance 7.8, recovery 8.5 conditional on final repairs, and performance 7.7 on the observed Mac—are acceptable editorial assessments. Performance and recovery need their stated conditions attached. Audio should remain ungraded in this pass. These numbers are neither a statistical scale nor an independent player panel's ratings.

## Findings that changed the audit

**Duplicate Appomattox was a real progression defect, now repaired in the checked route.** The initial public route recorded the finale at decisions 29 and 30. The missing terminal flag and early-arrival routing explained the duplicate. Making Appomattox terminal, refusing late commits and aligning the public commit guard is the right narrow repair. Focused tests cover all four finale choices, an early branch arrival, authentic old saved state and concluded-state rejection. I independently counted 29 decision entries and exactly one Appomattox entry in both `campaign-report-final.txt` and `campaign-cold-final.txt`; `campaign-route-final.json` contains 29 records. The final settlement says no further command is assigned. Post-repair headless evidence separately reports zero duplicate finales. The verification receipt initially miscounted the report as zero finales; that extraction error was corrected to one and I rechecked the underlying text.

**The Chancellorsville aftermath contradicted the selected strategy, now corrected for the checked B route.** I traced the original route's decisions 12 and 13: option B refused the split and formed a compact defensive line, but the next scene assumed Jackson's flank march and rupture. The new history-derived context helper has distinct B, C and D approaches, replaces descriptions, choices and consequences, and preserves IDs, proposers, effects and route targets. It does not rewrite old history or change battle results. The final public `chancellorsville-context-final.txt` reports the compact line and actual disciplined withdrawal; `chancellorsville-resolution-final.txt` and final chronicles preserve the coherent headquarters/consolidation decision and consequence. The second prose revision reads naturally and removes implementation commentary from the player flow. B/C/D and cold restoration have focused regression coverage. The unchanged flank approach and other outcome-dependent branches still need a wider causality review; a failed flank attack is a useful future test case, not a newly proven failure from this practice evidence alone.

**The Atlanta-to-Valley transition needed calendar orientation, now supplied in the checked handoff.** The initial route presented the alternate October–November 1864 Atlanta result before Third Winchester dated September 19. `atlanta-flashback-final.txt` and its screenshot visibly identify the reversal while preserving current strategic state. This bounded explanation does not make the resource chronology chronological or validate the election and commander-survival dependencies elsewhere. The main audit correctly retains the broader review.

**The alternate Gettysburg brief disagrees with its winning rule and remains open.** `jackson-play.png` shows a decisive victory while Culp's Hill remains Contested. The current briefing says to take both hills; the engine's option-A early success can instead follow surprise, coordinated attackers and disruption of the Union center before Hancock arrives. That is a confirmed explanation mismatch, not proof the decisive result was incorrect. A truthful brief is the appropriate bounded correction if the existing combat rule is being preserved. The actual winning route should also be legible to the player, rather than leaving the generic sector labels to imply a different requirement. The art pilot can be accepted with this documented known issue; it must not be described as repaired.

The final receipt set reports 1,343 gameplay tests, 242 current-art tests and 376 asset checks passing, plus successful native validation and build. I checked the receipt/log hashes; these remain candidate checks, not installed-state or human validation. The confirmed repairs and the remaining mission explanation take precedence over polishing the 10/10 roadmap, while the unfinished broader dependency review remains visible.

## Layout, performance and accessibility

The clean desktop image below confirms that the profiler overlay is not ordinary product UI. The field remains readable at this scale, but the inspector, toolbar, mission strip and persistent order receipt compete for space. Several other battlefields retain similar grass/road/foliage treatments despite their distinct landmarks. A coherent role/action family and stronger relationships between landmarks and surrounding terrain would improve the game more than unrelated additional asset batches.

Gettysburg reports 106 textures and 389.73 MiB of nominal RGBA pixels versus roughly 184–197 MiB on other observed fields. This is not measured GPU residency. Preloading several legacy/fallback families alongside native assets gives a plausible optimization target, but required contact/crew/casualty fallbacks must remain until their replacements are complete. Close active 1x windows have wall-interval p95 mainly around 21–23.5 ms; short windows can be perturbed by screenshot/control polling. Paused 8–10 ms profiles cannot establish active 60 fps. Scene-ready values around 253–281 ms exclude full cold navigation/network/decode costs.

Named Windows or integrated-graphics PC testing, physical-phone behavior, sustained active-play traces, actual memory measurements, fresh audio listening and complete keyboard/screen-reader flows are missing. The main audit now states these limits correctly. Development HMR interruption during ongoing edits is not sufficient evidence of a shipped product crash.

## Recommended order and acceptance standard

1. Finish and verify the confirmed terminal, branch-context, chronology-explanation and mission-brief repairs; then audit wider route causality and outcome dependencies.
2. Complete one coherent Gettysburg action/role family at overview, normal and close scale: matching contact/casualty states, actual bearers, artillery roles, missing Union bearings and controlled identities. Preserve strict complete fallback and save/reload behavior.
3. Improve command recovery around blocked turning and interrupted attacks. Keep the prior intent visible, make the next safe step clear, and let the player deliberately resume it. Preserve useful camera/display preferences.
4. Measure full active encounters and repeated battle loads on named target hardware before assigning a stronger performance grade. Separate cold load, frame delivery, interaction latency and real memory.
5. Test competent, varied plans for each mission and all relevant openings/ammunition bands. The observed failures at Manassas, Chancellorsville, Cedar Creek and Chickamauga merit targeted follow-up, not blanket easing of the rules.
6. Obtain informed historical/art review, fresh audio listening and uncoached input/accessibility testing before default promotion. Five uncoached players and an 80%/90-second task target are useful formative goals, not a usability certification or statistically stable benchmark.

The main audit's revised priorities and evidence limits are appropriate. Its final version should distinguish defects found from repairs verified, keep the failed original geometry gate visible and avoid equating tests, delegated simulations, sparse screenshots or model judgments with human experience. No default-art promotion, public release, complete-family approval or final installed-state acceptance is granted by this document.

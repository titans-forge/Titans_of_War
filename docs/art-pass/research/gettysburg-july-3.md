# Gettysburg, July 3: Focused Art Research Packet

**Prepared:** September 30, 2026<br>
**Production use:** Phase 1 of the Civil War focused art pass<br>
**Scenario:** Pickett-Pettigrew assault against Cemetery Ridge, July 3, 1863<br>
**Status:** Research-ready; individual regiment flag assignments still require a short artifact check before final export

## Production order

Use this packet in the following order. Do not begin with isolated soldier portraits or promotional close-ups.

1. **Lock the playable geography.** Preserve the relationship between Seminary Ridge, the open approach, Emmitsburg Road, Cemetery Ridge, the Angle, the Brian farm, and the Union rear around the Leister farm.
2. **Lock time, weather, light, and wind.** All later assets must share the same July 3 mid-afternoon lighting and calm-air treatment.
3. **Build the terrain and obstacle families.** Establish field variation, roads, farm lanes, fences, low stone walls, orchards, and woodlots before placing structures.
4. **Rebuild the Leister and Brian farm groups.** Use immediate post-battle photographs for silhouette and adjacency; use later surveys only for dimensions and construction clues.
5. **Rebuild the formation language.** Establish how ordered lines, fence crossings, fire, disorder, hand-to-hand contact, retreat, and losses change the visible human pattern.
6. **Finish uniforms, equipment, and role variants.** Officers, color bearers, infantry, artillery crews, and horse teams must read as different jobs at play scale.
7. **Finish flags.** Assign only documented or explicitly representative standards, then add cloth behavior consistent with the light wind.
8. **Finish artillery.** Distinguish iron rifles from bronze smoothbores, place crews by task, and give Cushing's battery progressive damage states.
9. **Add battle effects and damage.** Smoke, dust, impacts, broken fences, damaged equipment, and farm damage must follow the battle state rather than exist as permanent decoration.
10. **Run the historical-art gate.** Review every shipped asset for anachronisms, scale, camera, source traceability, play-scale readability, and performance.

## Evidence labels

- **Primary:** an 1863 photograph, contemporary map, official report, artifact, or contemporary observation.
- **Authoritative synthesis:** interpretation published by the National Park Service, Library of Congress, Smithsonian Institution, or U.S. Army Center of Military History.
- **Reconstruction reference:** a later measured survey, restored structure, current park view, or rehabilitated landscape.
- **Game-representative inference:** a visual decision needed by the game that is consistent with the evidence but is not directly documented.

Generated imagery is never historical evidence. It must be checked against the source ledger before integration.

## 1. Playable sector and visual hierarchy

The July 3 board represents the main Confederate assault from the Seminary Ridge side of the field toward the United States center on Cemetery Ridge. The park describes the battlefield as two low ridges separated by a mostly open agricultural landscape of rolling ground, cultivated fields, scattered woodlots, and farmsteads. The armies' main lines were nearly a mile apart. This supports a broad, readable farm landscape with subtle height changes rather than steep hills or a flat lawn. [NPS battlefield places](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/places.htm) and [NPS Gettysburg overview](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/gettysburg-overview.htm).

Major Charles Peyton's July 9 report describes Pickett's men advancing roughly 800 to 1,000 yards across open, lightly broken ground, crossing three high post-and-rail fences before reaching a forward Union line and the stone wall. Treat this as evidence for the Garnett/Pickett part of the attack, not as an exact measurement for every formation in the wider assault. [Peyton's official report, hosted by NPS](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/official-report-of-major-charles-s-peyton.htm).

The battlefield should read in three depth bands:

| Band | Required content | Visual intent |
|---|---|---|
| Confederate staging ground | Shallow Seminary Ridge crest, artillery positions, trampled assembly ground, batteries and limbers partly behind the crest | A prepared but exposed launch point, not a mountainous overlook |
| Approach | Open farm fields, small swales, crop and meadow variation, Emmitsburg Road, post-and-rail and worm fences, localized lanes and orchards | Long exposure, repeated obstacles, and increasing disorder |
| United States position | Cemetery Ridge, the low stone wall and Angle, guns, infantry line, Brian/Ziegler cover north of the Angle, Leister farm in the rear | A subtle defensive rise whose strength comes from fields of fire, cover, and organized resistance |

### Spatial anchors

- Cushing's position sat in a weed-choked pasture on Cemetery Ridge, partly enclosed by the stone wall at the Angle. The Emmitsburg Road ran a few hundred feet in front and roughly parallel to the ridge; the intervening plain was subdivided by wooden fences. [NPS: Alonzo Cushing at Gettysburg](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/alonzo-cushing-gettysburg.htm).
- The Angle's battle-era stone wall was originally about two to three feet high. It must not become a tall castle wall. [NPS: The Angle](https://www.nps.gov/places/the-angle.htm).
- The Brian farm stood on the northern portion of Cemetery Ridge at the edge of Ziegler's Grove. Its orchard and grove provided localized cover and concealment. [NPS: Abraham Brian Farm](https://home.nps.gov/places/abraham-brian-farm.htm) and the [NPS North Cemetery Ridge landscape assessment](https://parkplanning.nps.gov/showFile.cfm?projectID=32782&sfid=141254).
- The Leister farm was in the United States rear along Taneytown Road, approximately 300 yards east of the later High Water Mark area. It should be present as a rear headquarters and aid-station cluster, not placed directly on the stone wall. [NPS Leister Barn historic-structure record](https://irma.nps.gov/DataStore/Reference/Profile/2268157).

The 1864 Elliott map records roads, drainage, houses, vegetation, relief, field defenses, graves, and dead horses soon after the battle. Use it to check relative relationships, not as a literal crop or color map. [Library of Congress: Elliott's map of the battlefield](https://www.loc.gov/item/99447500/).

**Game-representative inference:** compress distance enough for play while preserving the order of features and the feeling of a long exposed advance. Avoid using scale compression to turn the Angle, Brian farm, and Leister farm into one crowded compound.

## 2. Weather, light, wind, and atmosphere

The strongest weather record is the Rev. Dr. Michael Jacobs observation reproduced in the U.S. Army Center of Military History's Gettysburg staff-ride briefing book. On July 3, the sky was fully covered by cumulo-stratus at 8 a.m.; by 2 p.m. only four-tenths remained covered, with summer cumulus or thundercloud. The recorded temperature reached 87 degrees Fahrenheit at 2 p.m. Wind was very gentle from the south-southwest. A thunderstorm was in the neighborhood at about 6 p.m., after the principal assault. [U.S. Army Center of Military History, *Gettysburg Staff Ride Briefing Book*, weather report pp. 44-45](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542/pdf/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542.pdf).

Peyton placed the Confederate artillery opening at about 1:30 p.m. and the infantry advance at about 2:30 p.m. [Peyton's official report](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/official-report-of-major-charles-s-peyton.htm).

### Art direction derived from the record

- Use **hot mid-afternoon summer light**, not dawn, sunset, or a uniformly gray overcast.
- Use a **blue-white sky with broken cumulus**, allowing alternating crisp and softened light across the field.
- Keep the color temperature natural. A warm dry field is appropriate; an orange cinematic grade is not.
- Use short-to-moderate shadows appropriate to mid-afternoon. The surviving record does not establish an exact game-camera shadow azimuth, so choose one stable direction and document it as a game-representative inference.
- Treat the air as **nearly calm**. Flags should often sag or form slow folds rather than remain fully extended.
- Let black-powder smoke collect and drift slowly. Do not sweep it away with strong wind, but clear it enough to preserve command readability.
- Contemporary reports mention heat, dry weather, and dust. Use localized road, marching, hoof, and gun dust; do not turn south-central Pennsylvania into a desert haze. [Eshleman's official report, hosted by NPS](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/official-report-of-major-benjamin-f-eshleman.htm).
- Do not add rain to the charge. The recorded thunderstorm was nearby later in the day.

## 3. Ground, crops, roads, fences, walls, and trees

### Ground and field pattern

The base terrain should be a varied working agricultural landscape:

- warm green and straw-green meadow;
- cut or trampled hay patches;
- standing and damaged crop plots where evidence supports them;
- dry exposed soil at paths, gateways, battery positions, and heavily crossed fences;
- weedier pasture around the Cemetery Ridge guns;
- shallow wheel ruts and compacted dirt on roads and farm lanes;
- localized orchard rows and woodlot edges rather than a uniform tree border.

The Brian farm grew wheat, barley, and hay and included meadow plus a small apple-and-peach orchard. That evidence supports crop variety around the Brian property, but it does not prove that every playable field contained the same crops or that modern field boundaries match 1863. [NPS Brian House 3D tour introduction](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/photosmultimedia/abraham-brian-house-3d.htm).

### Roads and lanes

- Render Emmitsburg Road and Taneytown Road as unpaved nineteenth-century roads with compacted earth, wheel tracks, uneven verges, and dust.
- Farm lanes should be narrower and visually subordinate.
- Do not copy modern asphalt, painted lines, curbs, tour-road shoulders, parking spaces, signs, or guardrails.
- Hancock Avenue and the dense memorial-road network belong to the commemorative landscape developed after the battle. They must not appear as 1863 roads. The NPS assessment distinguishes these commemorative avenues and monuments from the battlefield landscape. [NPS North Cemetery Ridge landscape assessment, pp. 58-59](https://parkplanning.nps.gov/showFile.cfm?projectID=32782&sfid=141254).

### Fences and walls

The central field needs more than one fence stamp:

- straight post-and-rail fences along field and road boundaries;
- Virginia worm or rider fencing in documented farm areas;
- low dry-laid stone walls;
- gates, gaps, removed rails, and battle damage;
- fence shadows and ground contact consistent with the board camera.

Peyton recalled three high post-and-rail fences in the charge lane. The NPS landscape assessment identifies stone walls, rider fences, and post fences associated with the Ziegler, Frey, and Brian farms. [Peyton report](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/official-report-of-major-charles-s-peyton.htm) and [NPS landscape assessment](https://parkplanning.nps.gov/showFile.cfm?projectID=32782&sfid=141254).

There is a useful source tension over the final stone wall: Peyton called part of it approximately breast-high, while the NPS describes the Angle wall as originally two to three feet high. The art should therefore use a low wall overall, with local height variation, field-stacked sections, and places strengthened by soldiers. It should not be shoulder-high everywhere.

Fence crossings are action states, not background decoration. Show men climbing, squeezing through gaps, dropping rails, bunching, and redressing their line after the obstacle. Do not animate entire formations phasing through intact fences.

### Trees, groves, and orchards

- Ziegler's Grove covered about 2.5 acres in 1863 and provided cover near the Brian property. Keep it a localized oak grove, not a forest wall. [NPS: Abraham Brian Farm](https://home.nps.gov/places/abraham-brian-farm.htm).
- The Brian orchard and Ziegler's Grove offered cover from shelling and concealment from observation. Trees should create a readable patch of cover while leaving the main charge lane open. [NPS landscape assessment](https://parkplanning.nps.gov/showFile.cfm?projectID=32782&sfid=141254).
- The later historical emphasis on the Copse of Trees as the single focal point of the charge was amplified after the war. Keep it visible but modest; do not make it a giant objective beacon. [NPS: On Freedom's Tenuous Edge virtual tour](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/photosmultimedia/on-freedom-s-tenuous-edge-virtual-tour.htm).
- Current tree cover cannot be copied wholesale. NPS notes that former fields became woods and that missing fields, fences, orchards, and lanes are being rehabilitated. [NPS battlefield rehabilitation goals](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/news/gett-rehab-goals.htm).

## 4. Lydia Leister farm art brief

### Evidence to preserve

- Lydia Leister's home was a small two-room log building used as George Meade's headquarters on July 2 and 3. [NPS preservation description](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/news/doors-open-2019.htm).
- The farm functioned as a busy headquarters with couriers, staff officers, headquarters guards, cavalry, and provost guards. [NPS: Lydia Leister Farm](https://www.nps.gov/places/lydia-leister-farm.htm).
- Overshooting Confederate shells struck the house and yard during the July 3 cannonade. Documented damage included the porch/steps and house fabric; staff horses tied at the fence were also hit. The headquarters moved, and the house and barn became an aid station and wounded-evacuation assembly point. [NPS: Lydia Leister Farm](https://www.nps.gov/places/lydia-leister-farm.htm).
- An immediate July 1863 photograph shows the farmhouse, dirt road, stone wall, yard, and dead horses after the fighting. It is the preferred exterior and adjacency reference. [Library of Congress: Meade's headquarters on Cemetery Ridge](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2018666296/).

### Build instructions

1. Base the exterior silhouette, roof pitch, door/window rhythm, low scale, yard, road edge, and wall/fence relationship on the July 1863 photograph.
2. Treat the house as whitewashed or light-painted only after checking the photograph, NPS description, and HABS material record together. The black-and-white photograph cannot prove an exact hue.
3. Build the farm as a group: house, barn, yard, fence, lane/road relationship, hitching area, and restrained domestic clutter.
4. Provide at least three states:
   - **headquarters intact:** staff movement, tied horses, guarded yard;
   - **under bombardment:** impact debris, frightened horses, disrupted staff, damaged fence and porch;
   - **post-cannonade aid station:** reduced command activity, wounded-evacuation cues, damage, and broken equipment.
5. Keep casualties restrained and readable. A few fallen horses may be historically grounded, but they should not become decorative gore.
6. Do not place Lydia Leister or her children at the farm during the battle; the family had left on July 1. [NPS: Lydia Leister Farm](https://www.nps.gov/places/lydia-leister-farm.htm).

### Reconstruction cautions

- The HABS record contains measured drawings and confirms pre-1863 construction, but the documentation was compiled after 1933. Use it for dimensions and construction clues, not as proof that every feature shown was present on July 3. [Library of Congress HABS PA-341](https://www.loc.gov/item/pa0014/).
- The current structure and furnished rooms include restoration and reproduction material. They are useful for material vocabulary, not a complete 1863 freeze-frame. [NPS: Lydia Leister Farm](https://www.nps.gov/places/lydia-leister-farm.htm).
- Exclude present monuments, interpretive cannon, signs, modern road treatment, and later additions.

## 5. Abraham Brian farm art brief

### Evidence to preserve

- Abraham Brian's wood-frame farmhouse was built in 1856. The farm occupied twelve acres at the northern end of Cemetery Ridge near Ziegler's Grove. [NPS preservation description](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/news/doors-open-gettysburg.htm) and [NPS Brian House 3D tour](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/photosmultimedia/abraham-brian-house-3d.htm).
- The farm supported wheat, barley, hay, meadow, garden crops, a small apple-and-peach orchard, livestock, a barn, an outdoor bake oven, post-and-rail fencing, worm fencing, and stone fencing. [NPS: Abraham Brian Farm](https://home.nps.gov/places/abraham-brian-farm.htm).
- Battery I, 5th U.S. Artillery, and elements of Hays's infantry used the Ziegler's Grove/Brian vicinity. [NPS: Abraham Brian Farm](https://home.nps.gov/places/abraham-brian-farm.htm).
- The house and barn were damaged by artillery and small-arms fire. The bake oven was gone, crops and orchard were ruined, 194 fence panels were lost, and stone from a fence had been removed for defenses. [NPS: Abraham Brian Farm](https://home.nps.gov/places/abraham-brian-farm.htm).
- The strongest visual reference is a July 1863 stereographic negative showing the house near the Second Corps line. The Library of Congress catalog uses the spelling “Bryan”; preserve its title in citations but use the park's “Brian” spelling in game text. [Library of Congress: the Bryan house near Pickett's Charge](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2018666298/).

### Build instructions

1. Use the July 1863 left and right plates to reconstruct the low farmhouse silhouette, openings, roof, yard, vegetation, fence, and neighboring-building relationship.
2. Create the farm as a connected defensive environment: house, barn/outbuilding mass, orchard edge, grove, fence gaps, low stonework, farm lane, and trampled ground.
3. Keep the orchard small and ordered enough to read as cultivated. Break rows where shelling and troop movement damaged them.
4. Provide progressive damage states: intact before heavy fire, bullet and shell damage during the fight, and heavily disrupted after the charge.
5. Keep the Brian family absent. They left because capture by Confederate forces threatened the freedom of Gettysburg's Black residents. [NPS Brian House 3D tour](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/photosmultimedia/abraham-brian-house-3d.htm).
6. Do not turn the farm into a plantation mansion, a large barn complex, or a pristine tourist property. Its visual power should come from a modest working farm caught inside a major battle.

### Reconstruction cautions

- Brian repaired the buildings and fences after the battle. Present appearances cannot be treated as unaltered 1863 evidence.
- The current NPS 3D tour is useful for scale and material study but documents a preserved/restored interior and exterior.
- The immediate photograph is monochrome and post-battle; use it for form and damage, not exact paint color or the pre-cannonade placement of movable objects.

## 6. Formations and human motion

The current scenario uses division- and brigade-scale abstractions: Pickett, Pettigrew, Trimble, Wilcox, and Alexander against Gibbon, Hays, Webb, Cushing, and Hancock. These names and strengths live in [the battle definitions](../../../src/battle/battleDefinitions.js). No single regiment's uniform or flag can represent an entire division as literal documentary truth.

The U.S. Army briefing book describes battle lines as normally two ranks deep, with skirmishers extended ahead and officers, file closers, and the color guard arranged around the line. It also notes that rifled-musket fire caused assault formations to loosen as soldiers sought cover, even though officers still needed sufficient close order for visual and verbal control. [U.S. Army Gettysburg Staff Ride Briefing Book, tactics pp. 42-43](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542/pdf/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542.pdf).

### Required visual states

| State | Human pattern |
|---|---|
| Assembly | Dressed lines, officers behind/front as appropriate, colors near formation center, limited idle motion |
| Advance | Two-rank impression at close scale, varied gait phase, maintained frontage, officers and colors anchoring direction |
| Fence approach | Compression, shortened stride, men raising weapons, visible anticipation of obstacle |
| Fence crossing | Climbing, ducking, gaps, temporary bunching, separated files, dropped or removed rails |
| Re-forming | Officers and color party become visible anchors; files spread and regain direction |
| Under fire | Local flinches, falls, gaps, crouching, smoke-hidden sections, formation edges becoming irregular |
| Firing | Aim, discharge, recoil, reload sequence, asynchronous volleys and individual fire |
| Final rush | Shorter spacing, forward lean, broken line, flags and officers still providing direction |
| Wall contact | Localized thrust, parry, clubbed weapon, surrender, recoil, and crowding at gaps; avoid a field-wide melee animation |
| Repulse | Small groups and individuals fall back at different speeds; formations lose identity rather than reverse-marching in perfect order |

Peyton's report specifically supports loading and firing during the advance, severe disruption near the wall, a localized hand-to-hand struggle, scattered survivors, and loss of regimental identity. [Peyton's official report](https://home.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/official-report-of-major-charles-s-peyton.htm).

**Game-representative inference:** visible soldier count and spacing should decline with game strength, but never imply a one-sprite-to-one-man scale. Each formation is a representative visual cluster.

## 7. Uniforms, equipment, and weapons

### United States troops

The Union base family should use the mid-war field silhouette: dark-blue wool sack coat, light or sky-blue trousers, and dark forage cap, with controlled use of frock coats and rank detail for officers and noncommissioned officers. The Army's uniform survey identifies the four-button blue wool sack coat, forage cap, and sky-blue trousers as defining field items. [U.S. Army Center of Military History: *Survey of U.S. Army Uniforms, Weapons and Accoutrements*](https://history.army.mil/html/museums/uniforms/survey_uwa.pdf).

Art treatment:

- use wool texture without glossy synthetic highlights;
- vary fading, dust, sweat-darkening, fit, cap posture, trouser tone, and equipment placement;
- keep the army visually cohesive while avoiding cloned figures;
- distinguish officers through cut, shoulder straps, sash or belt treatment, sword/revolver, and command posture rather than oversized gold ornament;
- preserve visible cartridge box, cap pouch, belt, canteen, and haversack where the camera permits.

### Confederate troops

Use a controlled family of gray, brown, and butternut cloth with some blue-trouser variation, differing jackets, forage caps and slouch hats, and uneven but serviceable equipment. The Smithsonian's Civil War object material records Confederate trouser colors including butternut, gray, and blue and preserves an 8th Virginia jacket as an individual artifact. This supports variation, not a license to make every soldier ragged or randomly colored. [Smithsonian: Soldier's Way of Life object group](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object-groups/civil-war-150/soldier-s-way-of-life).

Do not produce one identical pale-gray “Confederate uniform.” Also avoid the opposite caricature of every man in torn civilian clothing. Each generated batch needs a restrained palette and a declared represented formation or generic role.

### Infantry equipment and animation

The Army staff-ride reference describes .58-caliber U.S. rifled muskets and .577 Enfields as common muzzle-loading arms and gives the loading sequence: cartridge from box, cartridge opened, powder and bullet loaded, ramrod used, percussion cap placed, weapon cocked and fired. Use this sequence to make aim, fire, reload, and recovery silhouettes mechanically believable. [U.S. Army Gettysburg Staff Ride Briefing Book, small arms pp. 35-36](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542/pdf/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542.pdf).

- Rifles need realistic length relative to the body and a fine socket-bayonet silhouette.
- Ramrods should appear during loading, not remain magically attached to the hand.
- Cartridge boxes belong at the hip/back according to the strap arrangement; do not place modern pouches on chest rigs.
- Keep canteens and haversacks more common than full marching knapsacks during the final assault. The Army's Civil War art notes that soldiers commonly dropped knapsacks before action while retaining haversacks and canteens. [U.S. Army Center of Military History: Civil War art and uniform notes](https://history.army.mil/Research/Research-by-Conflict-and-Time-Period/Civil-War/CW-Art-and-Photos/).
- Use swords and revolvers primarily for officers. Avoid widespread pistol firing by line infantry.

## 8. Flags and color parties

### United States flags

Use a **34-star United States flag for July 3**. The 35th star for West Virginia became official on July 4, 1863. Flag makers used more than one star arrangement, so do not assume a single modern-looking grid without a chosen artifact reference. [Smithsonian flag chronology](https://avpreservation.si.edu/spotlight/flag-day/flag-facts?page=2), [Smithsonian 34-star flag object](https://n2t.net/ark:/65665/ng49ca746a3-1df2-704b-e053-15f76fa0b4fa), and [NPS Civil War Soldier guide: “34 Stars at Gettysburg”](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/teachers/lessonplans/Civil_War_Soldier_Guide-508.pdf).

The NPS guide explains that a full United States regiment might carry both national and regimental colors, while Confederate regiments generally carried one battle flag. Colors functioned as command and orientation points in smoke and noise. [NPS Civil War Soldier guide](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/teachers/lessonplans/Civil_War_Soldier_Guide-508.pdf).

### Confederate flags

Do not place the first Confederate national “Stars and Bars” over every formation. The Smithsonian notes that it was difficult to distinguish from the United States flag in battle and that the later battle flag became the principal Confederate combat emblem. [Smithsonian: The Flag in the Civil War](https://amhistory.si.edu/starspangledbanner/the-flag-in-the-civil-war.aspx).

The NPS's documented 13th Alabama flag is a Gettysburg-specific artifact tied to the July 3 assault. It was a Richmond Clothing Bureau Army of Northern Virginia bunting issue. Use it as a high-quality material, construction, wear, and proportion reference for the formation that explicitly represents the regiment; do not clone it onto Pickett's Virginia units or every Confederate division. [NPS: 13th Alabama Battle Flag](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/13th-alabama-battle-flag.htm).

### Production rule for division-scale formations

Before final export, each playable formation receives one of these labels in the asset manifest:

1. **Documented:** a particular regiment and surviving flag are named and sourced.
2. **Representative:** a sourced flag family appropriate to one of the formation's regiments is used, and the UI does not claim it is the division's single literal flag.
3. **Generic command cue:** a simplified side color is used without invented unit lettering or false historical specificity.

### Cloth behavior

- The recorded wind was very gentle. Use sag, fold travel, edge curl, and occasional partial extension.
- Never hold every flag as a flat rectangle.
- Keep the pole stable in the bearer's hands; cloth motion should lag turns and movement.
- Use different animation phases across formations.
- Add restrained tears, powder staining, and faded bunting only where supported by the chosen artifact.
- Preserve readable red, white, blue, and field patterns without neon saturation.
- In reduced-motion mode, hold a plausible draped shape rather than a rigid board.

## 9. Artillery and crew behavior

Cushing's Battery A, 4th U.S. Artillery entered the position with six 3-inch Ordnance Rifles. Its position included guns, limbers, horse teams, ammunition chests, and crews; the cannonade killed men and horses, shattered carriages and limbers, exploded a chest, and disabled pieces. Surviving guns were moved close to the wall and loaded with canister as the infantry approached. [NPS: Alonzo Cushing at Gettysburg](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/alonzo-cushing-gettysburg.htm).

The Army briefing book identifies the 3-inch Ordnance Rifle as an iron gun. It records that Union artillery at Gettysburg was mostly 10-pounder Parrotts and 3-inch Ordnance Rifles, while Confederate artillery was a mix dominated by 12-pounder bronze Napoleons, 10-pounder Parrotts, and 3-inch Ordnance Rifles. [U.S. Army Gettysburg Staff Ride Briefing Book, artillery pp. 37-40](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542/pdf/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542.pdf).

### Required artillery families

- **Cushing:** long, dark iron 3-inch Ordnance Rifle; intact, damaged, and last-gun states.
- **Alexander's command:** at least a weathered bronze Napoleon family and a dark iron rifle family; do not render the whole command as identical bronze guns.
- **Limber and caisson:** correct wheel scale, ammunition chests, spare wheel and tools, and horse-team relationship.
- **Crew:** chief of piece, gunner, sponge/rammer, ammunition carriers, vent/primer/lanyard roles, fuse work, drivers, and officers represented by distinct positions and motions.

The regulation drill placed eight crewmen with each gun, plus drivers. The load sequence moved ammunition from the limber to the muzzle, used sponge and rammer, serviced the vent and primer, and fired by lanyard. A full visual cluster may show fewer representative people at gameplay scale, but their poses must come from these jobs rather than random men standing around a cannon. [U.S. Army briefing book, artillery drill pp. 36-37](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542/pdf/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo92542.pdf).

### Effects

- Use a very brief muzzle flame and a larger white-gray black-powder smoke volume.
- Recoil should move the gun and provoke immediate crew response; avoid an exaggerated arcade jump.
- Use direct-fire alignment and barrel direction consistently.
- Reserve large fireballs for rare ammunition-chest or caisson events.
- Use canister at close range as a tactical state; do not render visible giant pellets crossing the whole field.
- Accumulate wheel ruts, churned turf, scattered tools, broken harness, and debris as the battery degrades.

## 10. Damage and battle-state progression

The board should not start in its final ruined state. Use phased environment and unit art:

| Phase | Field condition |
|---|---|
| Before cannonade | Intact fences and farm groups, dry field, orderly batteries, limited smoke, headquarters active |
| Cannonade | Dense but evolving smoke, shell impacts, frightened horses, damaged gun equipment, Leister-yard impacts, localized fires only if sourced |
| Assault begins | Long infantry lines, dust at movement and fence crossings, artillery smoke opening and closing sightlines |
| Mid-field disruption | Fence gaps, casualties, irregular lines, skirmisher contact, units redressing around colors and officers |
| Ridge contact | Heavy close-range smoke, canister effects, localized hand-to-hand struggle at wall/gaps, disabled Cushing pieces |
| Repulse | Broken Confederate groups, surrender clusters, wounded and abandoned equipment, damaged but still readable defensive line |

Avoid permanent looping destruction. A shell impact should create a short peak, a residue or damage decal, and then clear. Smoke must age from dense cream-white/gray to thin translucent haze.

## 11. Reconstruction and material caveats

| Risk | Required handling |
|---|---|
| Modern battlefield copied as 1863 | Use NPS rehabilitation studies to identify restored, missing, and commemorative features. Remove modern roads, monuments, towers, signs, fencing, parking, and utilities. |
| Current structures treated as untouched originals | Prioritize July 1863 photographs; use current views and HABS drawings only for scale and construction clues. |
| Black-and-white photographs used as color proof | Derive geometry and value relationships from photographs; establish color from material artifacts and documented construction. |
| Immediate post-battle photos treated as pre-battle layouts | Preserve damage evidence but allow for moved objects, burial work, missing rails, and battlefield cleanup. |
| Park cannon copied literally | Many displayed guns sit on later cast-iron carriages or commemorative positions. Model battle-era wood carriages from ordnance references. NPS explicitly identifies current Cushing guns as original cannon on cast-iron display carriages. [NPS Cushing page](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/historyculture/cushing-at-gettysburg.htm). |
| Farm plots over-specified | Brian crop evidence is property-specific. Do not assign exact crop species to every field without a plot-level cultural-landscape source. |
| Copse of Trees exaggerated | Keep it modest and contextual; its status as the singular target was amplified after the battle. |
| Division given one “exact” flag | Mark it as documented, representative, or generic in the manifest. Never invent a division flag. |
| Paintings used as literal records | Period illustrations and the Cyclorama are valuable for motion and atmosphere but remain composed artworks. Cross-check geometry and equipment before use. |
| Generated lettering and insignia accepted by eye | Rebuild stars, numerals, devices, trim, and lettering as controlled vector or hand-corrected layers. |

## 12. Ready-to-use visual briefs

These briefs constrain concept generation. They are not final prompts and do not replace the evidence links.

### Terrain base

> Elevated three-quarter, horizonless 2D battlefield terrain representing the open agricultural ground between low Seminary and Cemetery Ridges at Gettysburg on July 3, 1863; hot mid-afternoon light, broken summer cumulus, warm green and straw pasture, cultivated plots, dry dirt roads and farm lanes, shallow swales, restrained woodlots, post-and-rail and worm fencing, low irregular stone walls, realistic scale and muted natural color; no monuments, paved roads, signs, modern buildings, fantasy cliffs, saturated game-board grass, or visible horizon.

### Leister farm group

> Small low two-room whitewashed log farmhouse and farm group used as Meade's headquarters, elevated three-quarter view matching battlefield camera; battle-era dirt Taneytown Road edge, stone and wood fencing, modest yard, barn, tied horses and staff activity; produce intact, bombardment-damaged, and aid-station variants based on the July 1863 Gardner photograph; no memorial cannon, tour signs, asphalt, modern additions, grand architecture, or invented Victorian decoration.

### Brian farm group

> Modest 1856 wood-frame two-room farmstead on northern Cemetery Ridge, elevated three-quarter view; small house, barn/outbuilding relationship, apple-and-peach orchard edge, wheat/barley/hay field cues, post-and-rail and worm fencing, low stonework, nearby oak grove; produce intact and battle-damaged states based on the July 1863 Library of Congress stereograph; no plantation mansion, modern restoration polish, monuments, asphalt, or family civilians during the battle.

### United States infantry family

> Mid-1863 Army of the Potomac infantry at Gettysburg, dark-blue wool sack coats, light-blue trousers, dark forage caps, worn leather cartridge boxes and belts, canteens and haversacks, long rifled muskets and socket bayonets; varied realistic faces, body types, fit, fading, sweat and dust; ready, advance, aim, fire, reload, fence-crossing, rally, wall-defense and exhausted states; historically serious, natural proportions, no rigid formation stamp, anime faces, plastic fabric, modern gear, oversized weapons, or identical synchronized poses.

### Confederate infantry family

> Army of Northern Virginia infantry in the July 3 Gettysburg assault, controlled range of gray, brown and butternut jackets and trousers with some blue-trouser variation, forage caps and slouch hats, worn serviceable equipment, rifled muskets and socket bayonets; coherent regimental palette within each batch; advance, fence-crossing, fire-and-reload, final rush, wall contact, surrender and scattered retreat states; no identical pale-gray clones, theatrical rags on every soldier, bright modern colors, anime styling, plastic fabric, or generic flag baked into the figures.

### Cushing battery family

> Battery A, 4th U.S. Artillery at the Angle, six-gun family of long dark iron 3-inch Ordnance Rifles on battle-era wood carriages, crew roles placed by authentic drill, limber and ammunition chest behind, horse-team cues, low stone wall and weed-choked pasture; intact, cannonade-damaged and final canister-defense variants; no bronze Napoleon substituted for Cushing's rifles, cast-iron memorial carriages, random idle crew ring, oversized fireball, or clean parade-ground finish.

### Flag motion study

> Battle-worn wool bunting on a stable staff in very gentle wind, sequence from draped sag through slow traveling fold and partial extension to edge curl, consistent elevated camera and mid-afternoon light, bearer hands maintaining pole contact, restrained wear and fading; no flat rectangle, rigid board, strong-wind horizontal streaming, mirrored devices, invented lettering, modern 50-star canton, or neon color.

## 13. Acceptance gate before image integration

- [ ] The feature can be located in at least one direct source in this packet.
- [ ] Primary, synthesis, reconstruction, and inference are not conflated in the manifest.
- [ ] Camera, scale, sun direction, shadow softness, and palette match the approved terrain base.
- [ ] No monument, memorial road, tour infrastructure, modern fence, utility, or replica carriage has slipped into the 1863 scene.
- [ ] Gettysburg's ridges remain low and the approach remains long and exposed.
- [ ] Emmitsburg and Taneytown roads read as unpaved roads.
- [ ] Fence types vary by evidence and have visible ground contact.
- [ ] The Angle wall is low and irregular rather than fortification-scale.
- [ ] Leister and Brian structures match immediate-post-battle silhouettes at game camera angle.
- [ ] Leister and Brian surroundings are connected farm environments rather than isolated building stickers.
- [ ] Uniform families are coherent but not cloned.
- [ ] Weapons, bayonets, straps, pouches, canteens, and artillery tools are structurally plausible.
- [ ] A 34-star United States flag is used for July 3.
- [ ] Confederate flags are assigned through the documented/representative/generic rule.
- [ ] Flags sag and fold appropriately in very gentle wind.
- [ ] Cushing uses 3-inch Ordnance Rifles and has progressive battery damage.
- [ ] Hand-to-hand action is localized to actual contact at walls and gaps.
- [ ] Damage appears progressively and effects decay.
- [ ] The asset remains readable at normal play zoom and credible in a close promotional crop.
- [ ] Source URL, source type, prompt, edits, dimensions, rights note, version, and final hash are recorded.

### Deliberately excluded as controlling evidence

- commercial battlefield blogs and unsourced reconstructions;
- modern reenactor photographs as proof of exact Gettysburg material culture;
- film and television costume design;
- current satellite imagery without the cultural-landscape correction;
- AI-generated references;
- a painting or Cyclorama detail used alone to establish geometry, color, or equipment.

These may inspire composition only after the authoritative evidence above has settled the object, date, and place.
